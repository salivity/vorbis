What Sample Rates Does Vorbis Audio Support?

The Ogg Vorbis audio format accommodates an extensive range of sampling frequencies, formally spanning from 8 kHz telephony audio up to 192 kHz high-resolution digital master files. In typical consumer deployments, streaming services, and game audio pipelines, Vorbis is most commonly utilized between 16 kHz and 48 kHz, with 44.1 kHz (CD quality) and 48 kHz (digital media standard) serving as the predominant configurations.

Specification Capabilities

According to the official Xiph.Org specification, the Vorbis format stores the sample rate as an unsigned 32-bit integer field within its primary identification header. Because of this bitstream design, the underlying architecture does not enforce rigid, legacy sample-rate tables like older lossy codecs:

  • Lower End (Telephony & Voice): Designed to scale down to 8 kHz (and lower custom rates) for bandwidth-constrained voice recording.
  • Standard Operating Range: Standard everyday implementations regularly encode between 16 kHz, 22.05 kHz, 32 kHz, 44.1 kHz, and 48 kHz.
  • High-End Studio Masters: Officially designed to scale up to 96 kHz and 192 kHz for multi-channel, high-resolution source material.

Real-World Encoder Implementations

Although the format itself is nearly arbitrary regarding frequency definitions, specific implementations such as libvorbis optimize their psychoacoustic models, floor curves, and codebooks for maximum fidelity across mainstream sample rates. Standard media players and encoding frontends routinely default to 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz, where the perceptual bitrate-to-quality ratio remains most efficient.
