Vorbis Frequency to Bark Scale Formula

The Vorbis audio codec utilizes psychoacoustic models to eliminate imperceptible audio data, which requires converting linear frequencies measured in Hertz into Bark scale critical bands that reflect human auditory perception. This article provides the exact mathematical formula used by the Vorbis specification to map linear frequencies to Bark units, breaks down its individual components, and explains its implementation within Vorbis audio encoding.

The Vorbis Bark Mapping Formula

According to the official Vorbis I specification, the function used to convert a linear frequency \(f\) (in Hertz) into Bark units is defined as:

\[\text{Bark}(f) = 13.1 \cdot \arctan(0.00074 \cdot f) + 2.24 \cdot \arctan(1.85 \times 10^{-8} \cdot f^2) + 10^{-4} \cdot f\]

In standard source implementations (such as floor0.c in libvorbis), the expression is evaluated directly using standard C library math functions:

bark = 13.1 * atan(0.00074 * f) + 2.24 * atan(f * f * 1.85e-8) + 1e-4 * f;

Breakdown of the Equation

The formula is an empirical approximation tailored to closely fit the classic Bark critical band curves defined by Eberhard Zwicker, while remaining continuous and computationally efficient across the audible spectrum (0 Hz to 22,050 Hz and beyond):

  1. Low-to-Mid Frequency Curvature: \(13.1 \cdot \arctan(0.00074 \cdot f)\)
    At low frequencies (below roughly 1 kHz), the auditory system exhibits roughly linear critical bandwidths. The first arctangent term provides steep, near-linear growth at the lower end before leveling off into logarithmic scaling through the midrange.

  2. High-Frequency Curvature: \(2.24 \cdot \arctan(1.85 \times 10^{-8} \cdot f^2)\)
    The second term acts on the square of the frequency. It remains negligible at low and moderate frequencies but introduces additional expansion above roughly 5 kHz to model the widening of critical bands at the upper range of human hearing.

  3. High-Frequency Linear Compensation: \(10^{-4} \cdot f\)
    Because the arctangent functions asymptotically approach finite limits as frequency increases toward infinity, the small linear term ensures that the mapping remains strictly monotonic and continues to increase at very high sample rates.

Role in the Vorbis Codec

Vorbis primarily utilizes this Bark conversion within its psychoacoustic noise-masking engine and its spectral envelope representations:

  • Floor Type 0: The Floor 0 spectral envelope generator explicitly spaces its Line Spectral Frequency (LSF) coefficients on a Bark scale using this exact formula, prioritizing spectral resolution where human ears are most sensitive.
  • Psychoacoustic Masking: Masking curves generated during encoding are calculated in Bark domain units so that energy thresholds match the critical band filtering performed by the human cochlea.
