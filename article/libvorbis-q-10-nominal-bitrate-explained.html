Libvorbis -q 10 Nominal Bitrate Explained

This article examines the nominal bitrate associated with the highest quality setting, -q 10, in the libvorbis audio encoder. It outlines the exact bitrate target, how the variable bitrate (VBR) mechanism behaves at this level, and where this setting fits within the overall Vorbis quality scale.

The Nominal Bitrate for -q 10

In libvorbis, the quality setting -q 10 corresponds to a nominal bitrate of 500 kbps (kilobits per second) for a standard stereo track.

This represents the maximum official quality setting provided by the standard reference encoder.

How libvorbis Quality Mapping Works

Vorbis encodes audio using Variable Bitrate (VBR) by default. Instead of forcing a specific bitrate, the encoder targets a fidelity level defined by the -q parameter, which generally ranges from -1 (lowest) to 10 (highest). Fractional values (such as 9.5) are also valid.

Below is the standard nominal bitrate mapping for stereo audio across key quality levels:

  • -q 0: ~64 kbps
  • -q 1: ~80 kbps
  • -q 2: ~96 kbps
  • -q 3: ~112 kbps
  • -q 4: ~128 kbps
  • -q 5: ~160 kbps (perceptual transparency for most listeners)
  • -q 6: ~192 kbps
  • -q 7: ~224 kbps
  • -q 8: ~256 kbps
  • -q 9: ~320 kbps
  • -q 10: ~500 kbps

Behavior and Characteristics of -q 10

  • Dynamic Range: Because Vorbis is a true VBR format, a file encoded at -q 10 will not stay strictly at 500 kbps. Simple passages (such as silence or solo instruments) may drop well below 400 kbps, while dense, complex passages can exceed 500 kbps.
  • Diminishing Returns: For almost all listening environments and equipment, perceptual transparency (indistinguishability from uncompressed source audio) is reached between -q 5 (~160 kbps) and -q 7 (~224 kbps).
  • Use Case: Using -q 10 minimizes compression artifacts to the theoretical limit of the Vorbis format. However, it results in substantially larger file sizes, often approaching roughly half the size of uncompressed 16-bit/44.1 kHz PCM audio. If fidelity higher than -q 8 or -q 9 is strictly required, lossless codecs like FLAC are typically preferred over lossy compression at 500 kbps.

Example Usage

To encode audio to -q 10 using FFmpeg:

ffmpeg -i input.wav -c:a libvorbis -q:a 10 output.ogg

To encode audio using the reference oggenc tool:

oggenc -q 10 input.wav -o output.ogg
