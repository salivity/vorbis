Vorbis Comment Field for Track Lyrics

This article explains the standard Vorbis comment field name used to store track lyrics in modern audio formats like Ogg Vorbis, FLAC, and Opus. You will learn the exact field identifier to use, how the tagging standard handles plain text and synchronized lyrics, and common compatibility considerations across media players and metadata editors.

The standard and most widely accepted Vorbis comment field name for storing track lyrics is LYRICS.

Vorbis comments are case-insensitive by specification, meaning that writing LYRICS, Lyrics, or lyrics is technically valid. However, standard convention dictates using all uppercase characters to ensure consistency across different tag editors, software libraries, and hardware players.

Text Formatting and Character Encoding

Vorbis comments natively support UTF-8 encoding. Because of this, the LYRICS field supports:

  • Multi-language scripts, including non-Latin alphabets and accented characters.
  • Standard line breaks (\n or \r\n), allowing lyrics to be displayed line-by-line rather than as a single block of continuous text.

Synchronized vs. Unsynchronized Lyrics

The LYRICS tag is primarily used to store plain, unsynchronized text. However, many media players (such as foobar2000 and various mobile players) also parse time-stamped LRC format within the LYRICS field to display scrolling, synchronized text during playback.

While some legacy or proprietary applications historically introduced alternative tags like UNSYNCEDLYRICS (derived from the ID3v2 standard) or SYNCEDLYRICS, modern tagging suites—including MusicBrainz Picard and Mp3tag—standardize on LYRICS for Vorbis-based containers. Storing your track lyrics under the LYRICS tag guarantees the broadest compatibility across modern operating systems, digital audio workstations, and software audio players.
