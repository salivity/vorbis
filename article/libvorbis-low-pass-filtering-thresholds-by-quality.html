Libvorbis Low-Pass Filtering Thresholds by Quality

This article examines the low-pass filtering thresholds utilized by the standard Xiph.Org libvorbis audio encoder across its variable quality levels (ranging from -q -1 to -q 10). Because lossy compression must balance bitrate constraints against audible fidelity, libvorbis strategically curtails high-frequency content to conserve bits for perceptually critical mid- and low-range frequencies. Below is a detailed technical reference outlining the nominal low-pass cutoff frequencies applied to standard 44.1 kHz stereo audio, how these thresholds change with quality settings, and how the internal filtering architecture operates.

The Purpose of Low-Pass Filtering in Vorbis

Psychoacoustic models dictate that human hearing is far less sensitive to fine details at the upper limit of human perception (above 15 kHz to 20 kHz). Encoding these frequencies requires a substantial amount of bandwidth due to the high density of rapid zero-crossings and energy transients.

By applying a low-pass filter prior to quantization and MDCT (Modified Discrete Cosine Transform) processing, libvorbis eliminates audio content that requires disproportionate bit allocation. This allows the encoder to preserve clean mid-range definition and prevent ringing or artifacting in the most audible bands.

Low-Pass Cutoffs for 44.1 kHz Stereo Audio

In standard libvorbis configurations (such as standard release 1.3.x and earlier 1.x branch tunings defined in modes/setup_44.h), nominal target cutoffs scale progressively alongside the nominal quality values. For a standard 44.1 kHz stereo source, the approximate low-pass threshold for each integer quality level is as follows:

Quality Setting Nominal Bitrate Approximate Low-Pass Filter Cutoff
-q -1 ~45 kbps ~10.5 kHz
-q 0 ~64 kbps ~11.5 kHz
-q 1 ~80 kbps ~12.5 kHz
-q 2 ~96 kbps ~13.5 kHz
-q 3 ~112 kbps ~15.0 kHz
-q 4 ~128 kbps ~16.0 kHz
-q 5 ~160 kbps ~17.5 kHz
-q 6 ~192 kbps ~18.5 kHz
-q 7 ~224 kbps ~19.5 kHz
-q 8 ~256 kbps ~20.0 kHz
-q 9 ~320 kbps > 20.0 kHz / Minimal filtering
-q 10 ~500 kbps Full spectrum (up to 22.05 kHz Nyquist)

Non-Integer Values

libvorbis accepts fractional quality settings (such as -q 4.5 or -q 6.2). The internal configuration tables linearly interpolate the psychoacoustic thresholds, resulting in a proportional filter cutoff between the nearest defined profile modes.

Technical Mechanics of the Filter

The cutoff frequencies implemented in libvorbis do not act like brick-wall Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) filters. Instead, the behavior involves:

  1. Soft Rolloff: The psychoacoustic masking curve slopes downward across transition bands rather than cutting audio off sharply. This minimizes pre-echo and phase distortion around the cutoff frequency.
  2. Frequency Floor Coupling: Beyond the defined cutoff, high-frequency energy is typically cleared from the spectral floor completely, zeroing out MDCT bins that exceed the target band.
  3. Sample Rate Adjustments: When processing inputs at other sample rates (such as 48 kHz, 32 kHz, or 22 kHz), the absolute cutoff is adjusted. For higher rates like 48 kHz, the relative thresholds scale up slightly, though standard human auditory ceilings (approximately 20 kHz) remain the maximum applied threshold regardless of higher sample rates.

Disabling Low-Pass Filters

In standard standard CLI encoders (such as oggenc), the low-pass filter is dynamically engaged by default based on the -q parameter. Advanced users can manually override or adjust these behaviors via specific frontend arguments (such as --advanced-encode-option parameters), though bypassing low-pass filtering at low bitrates will substantially degrade overall audio fidelity due to bit starvation in lower frequency bands.
