Highest Sample Rate Tested with libvorbis

The libvorbis audio codec is technically capable of supporting a vast range of sample rates due to its open and flexible architecture. While the underlying Vorbis I bitstream specification technically permits sample rates up to the 32-bit unsigned integer maximum of roughly 4.29 GHz, the highest officially validated and tested sample rate within the standard reference encoder implementation (libvorbis) is 192 kHz. This article details the testing limits of libvorbis, how the codec handles high-resolution audio, and why practical tests rarely exceed this threshold.

Standard Supported Sample Rates

In standard production environments, libvorbis is formally tuned and qualified for sample rates between 8 kHz and 192 kHz. The development team at Xiph.Org explicitly designed and tested the standard psychoacoustic profiles up to 192 kHz to support high-definition audio formats (such as 24-bit/192 kHz studio master recordings). Across the official documentation and test suites, 192,000 Hz represents the highest standard sample rate subjected to systematic performance, fidelity, and quality tuning.

Bitstream vs. Implementation Limits

There is a distinct difference between what the Vorbis bitstream format allows and what the libvorbis reference encoder actively supports:

  • Format Specification Limit: The Vorbis stream header stores the sample rate as a 32-bit unsigned integer (vorbis_info.rate). Consequently, the raw bitstream structure theoretically supports any sample rate from 1 Hz up to 4,294,967,295 Hz (~4.29 GHz).
  • Encoder Implementation Limits: In practical testing, feeding arbitrary rates higher than 192 kHz into libvorbis produces varying results. Developers and researchers working on ultrasonic recording—such as capturing bat vocalizations or underwater acoustic signals—have experimentally pushed libvorbis to encode data at 384 kHz and 500 kHz. However, these rates bypass the codec's calibrated psychoacoustic tables.

Why Testing Caps at 192 kHz

Testing higher sample rates provides diminishing returns for standard lossy compression:

  1. Psychoacoustic Models: Vorbis is a perceptual audio codec designed to eliminate sounds the human ear cannot perceive. Human hearing tops out around 20 kHz. At a 192 kHz sample rate, the Nyquist frequency allows capture up to 96 kHz—far beyond human perception. Higher rates lack optimized perceptual quantization tables.
  2. Frequency Masking: The floor and residue curves in libvorbis are tuned against the Bark scale. Beyond 192 kHz, libvorbis cannot effectively model perceptual masking, often defaulting to less efficient generic quantization that behaves more like raw data compression than optimized perceptual encoding.

In conclusion, while experimental scientific users have fed ultrasonic data up to 384 kHz and beyond through custom configurations, 192 kHz remains the highest sample rate ever systematically and successfully tested with full encoder tuning in official libvorbis development.
