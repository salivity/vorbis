Tagging Vorbis Files with MusicBrainz Picard

MusicBrainz Picard fully supports tagging Vorbis audio files (commonly found in the .ogg container format). This guide covers how Picard utilizes Vorbis comments for metadata storage, how to properly scan and identify your Vorbis tracks using the MusicBrainz database, and the step-by-step process for embedding accurate tags and album artwork into your audio files.

Native Support for Vorbis Comments

Vorbis files do not use ID3 tags like MP3 files. Instead, they rely on a native metadata system called Vorbis comments. MusicBrainz Picard has native, out-of-the-box support for Vorbis comments and automatically maps MusicBrainz database fields—such as artist, album, track title, release date, and catalog numbers—directly to their standard Vorbis equivalents without requiring extra plugins or configuration.

Picard also supports embedding cover art into Vorbis files using the standardized METADATA_BLOCK_PICTURE format, ensuring compatibility across modern software players and hardware devices.

How to Tag Vorbis Files Using Picard

Tagging your Vorbis collection with Picard involves four straightforward steps:

1. Load the Audio Files

Open MusicBrainz Picard and click Add Files or Add Folder to import your Vorbis (.ogg) tracks. The files will appear in the left pane under "Unclustered Files."

2. Group and Identify the Tracks

Picard offers multiple ways to match your files against the MusicBrainz database:

  • Cluster: Select your files and click Cluster to group files by their existing metadata into albums.
  • Lookup: Select a cluster or individual tracks and click Lookup to search the database using the existing textual metadata.
  • Scan: If your files lack metadata or have incorrect tags, select the files and click Scan. Picard will generate an AcoustID audio fingerprint for each track and match it against the database based on the actual audio content.

3. Review Matches

Once identified, the tracks will move to the right-hand pane under their respective album entries:

  • Green icons indicate strong, reliable matches.
  • Yellow or orange icons indicate potential discrepancies that may require manual verification.
  • Red icons indicate poor matches.

Select a track to compare the "Original Value" against the "New Value" in the bottom metadata panel. If a track is mismatched, you can drag and drop it to the correct slot in the album listing.

4. Save the Tags

Once the tags and artwork look correct, select the album or tracks in the right-hand pane and click Save. Picard writes the Vorbis comments and embedded artwork directly to your files. If you have file-renaming rules configured in Picard’s options, it will also rename and reorganize the files according to your preferences.
