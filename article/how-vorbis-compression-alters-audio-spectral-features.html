How Vorbis Compression Alters Audio Spectral Features

Vorbis is an open-source, lossy audio coding format that reduces file sizes by removing acoustic information deemed imperceptible to the human auditory system. During this process, the audio signal undergoes a transformation into the frequency domain via the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT). Consequently, compressing audio with Vorbis directly impacts key spectral characteristics, including spectral envelope precision, bandwidth limits, harmonic integrity, and the distribution of noise energy across critical frequency bands.

High-Frequency Attenuation and Spectral Roll-Off

One of the most noticeable spectral alterations introduced by Vorbis is dynamic bandwidth limitation. At low to medium bitrates, Vorbis employs a low-pass filter to eliminate high-frequency content above a certain threshold (typically between 15 kHz and 20 kHz, depending on the chosen quality setting). In frequency analysis, this causes a sharp downward shift in the spectral roll-off—the frequency below which a specified percentage of total spectral energy is contained—and lowers the overall spectral centroid of the audio.

Spectral Envelope Simplification

Vorbis separates spectral data into two components: the spectral floor (the general shape or envelope of the spectrum) and the spectral residue (the fine harmonic details). The spectral floor is modeled using a piecewise linear representation (Floor 1) or vector quantization of line spectral pairs (Floor 0). This abstraction smooths the overall spectral shape, eliminating subtle variations in the original frequency contour and mildly flattening natural peaks across broader frequency spans.

Harmonic Structure and Residue Quantization

After modeling the envelope, the remaining fine structure (residue) is quantized. Vorbis groups MDCT coefficients into bands modeled after psychoacoustic critical bands. Lower-energy harmonics falling near prominent tonal components are subjected to aggressive quantization or omitted entirely if they fall below the calculated masking threshold. As a result, the sharpness of harmonic peaks can be dulled, harmonic-to-noise ratios (HNR) are lowered, and low-energy partials may disappear completely.

Redistribution of Quantization Noise

Rather than spreading quantization error uniformly across the spectrum, Vorbis shapes the noise floor to mirror the simultaneous masking characteristics of the human ear. Noise is intentionally injected into frequency bands that contain loud, dominant components where human hearing is least sensitive to distortion. This alters the spectral flatness measure, artificially raising the local noise floor in active frequency regions while keeping silent or exposed regions clean.

Time-Frequency Resolution Smearing

Vorbis mitigates transient distortion using variable-length MDCT windows—typically 2048 samples for steady-state signals and 256 samples for fast transients. However, the switching process is not instantaneous. During complex signals containing dense polyphony or sharp percussive attacks, window compromises can cause subtle temporal-spectral smearing (often manifesting as pre-echo). This smearing redistributes frequency energy across adjacent temporal frames, slightly diffusing the frequency definition of rapid, dynamic sounds.
