Vorbis Support for Arbitrary Sample Rates

This article explores whether the Vorbis audio compression format supports encoding audio at arbitrary or non-standard sampling rates, such as 22,050 Hz or other irregular frequencies. It examines the technical design defined in the Vorbis specification, how the reference encoder handles varied sample rates, and practical playback compatibility considerations.

Vorbis Specification and Sample Rate Storage

The Vorbis bitstream specification natively supports arbitrary sample rates. In the Vorbis identification header, the sample rate is stored as an unsigned 32-bit integer (audio_sample_rate). Because of this design, the format is not restricted to a fixed list of predefined frequencies like some legacy codecs (such as early MP3 profiles). The bitstream technically allows any integer sampling rate between 1 Hz and \(2^{32} - 1\) Hz (approximately 4.29 GHz), easily accommodating standard rates, common downsampled rates like 22,050 Hz, and completely non-standard rates such as 12,345 Hz.

Standard Sub-Multiples vs. Truly Arbitrary Rates

A rate of 22,050 Hz is an industry-standard sub-multiple of the 44,100 Hz CD audio rate. Vorbis has full, first-class tuning for 22,050 Hz across all encoders.

When dealing with truly arbitrary, non-standard rates (e.g., 23,456 Hz or 31,000 Hz), Vorbis still succeeds at encoding. The reference library (libvorbis) uses internal tuning templates based on psychoacoustic models calibrated for standard rates (such as 8, 11.025, 16, 22.05, 32, 44.1, 48, 88.2, and 96 kHz). When an arbitrary sample rate is supplied, the encoder maps or interpolates internal analysis settings to the closest available tuning profile while tagging the bitstream with the exact user-specified sample rate.

Encoder and Decoder Behavior

  • libvorbis and oggenc: The standard command-line tool oggenc and applications using libvorbis accept non-standard sample rates without requiring pre-resampling. The encoder will construct the appropriate floor configurations and frequency vectors matching the actual input rate.
  • Decoders: Any compliant Vorbis decoder will read the exact sample rate from the identification header and output the decompressed PCM data at that precise frequency.

Playback and Hardware Considerations

While the Vorbis format and decoders handle arbitrary sampling rates seamlessly, the target playback system may introduce limitations. Audio hardware (DACs) and operating system audio mixers generally operate on standard clock frequencies (e.g., 44,100 Hz, 48,000 Hz, 96,000 Hz). When playing a file with an uncommon rate:

  1. The decoder outputs raw PCM at the non-standard rate.
  2. The operating system's software mixer or audio server automatically resamples the stream to the hardware's native output rate.

In summary, Vorbis fully supports encoding and decoding at arbitrary sampling rates, whether they are standard reductions like 22,050 Hz or entirely custom frequencies.
