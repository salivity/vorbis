How Vorbis Bitstreams Signal End of Stream

This article explains how a Vorbis audio stream signals its conclusion within a physical bitstream. It details the structural separation between the raw Vorbis codec packets and the underlying Ogg container format, the specific header flags used to mark the termination of data, and how decoders determine the exact ending sample of an audio track.

The Container-Level Mechanism

Vorbis is a raw packet-based audio codec and does not manage physical transport, multiplexing, or physical framing on its own. Instead, it relies on an encapsulation layer—almost universally the Ogg bitstream format. Because of this architecture, the signaling of the end of a physical stream is executed entirely at the Ogg container layer rather than within the compressed Vorbis audio packets themselves.

The End of Stream (EOS) Flag

An Ogg bitstream is divided into distinct units called pages. Each Ogg page begins with a 27-byte capture pattern and header, which includes a 1-byte field named header_type_flag. Stream termination is explicitly signaled using a single bit within this field:

  • Bit Value 0x04 (eos): Setting this bit designates the page as the "End of Stream."

When a multiplexer or encoder writes the final Vorbis packet of a logical stream, it places that packet (or its final segment) into an Ogg page and sets the 0x04 bit in the header. Once an Ogg-compliant decoder encounters a page with the EOS flag set, it knows that no further pages belong to that specific logical bitstream.

Exact Truncation and Granule Position

Because Vorbis utilizes the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) with overlapping windows, audio blocks cannot abruptly stop; they naturally produce trailing samples of overlap. To signal the exact ending sample without trailing silence or encoder delay distortion, the EOS page utilizes the header's 8-byte granulepos (granule position) field.

  1. PCM Sample Counter: For Vorbis, the granulepos represents the absolute index of the decoded PCM sample up to that point.
  2. Decoder Truncation: On the page marked with the EOS flag, the granulepos specifies the exact end of the intended audio data. The decoder uses this value to discard any trailing overlap samples generated by the final packet's inverse transform, ensuring seamless, sample-accurate audio playback.

Chained Physical Bitstreams

A physical Ogg Vorbis stream can contain multiple sequential logical streams, a structure known as chaining. In a chained bitstream, the physical media continues beyond an EOS marker. The arrival of an Ogg page with the 0x04 (eos) flag terminates the current logical stream, closes the active Vorbis decoder instance, and signals the container reader to look for a subsequent page carrying the 0x02 (bos / Beginning of Stream) flag to initialize the next sequence. If the physical stream ends (such as the end of a file or connection close) immediately after an EOS page, the entire physical bitstream is concluded.
