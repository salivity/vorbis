Libvorbis Stereo Coupling Aggression Controls

Libvorbis utilizes multichannel coupling to maximize encoding efficiency by identifying redundancies between audio channels. This article covers the internal psychoacoustic parameters and tuning structures within libvorbis that dictate the aggression of stereo coupling, particularly the transition between lossless mid/side coupling and lossy point stereo coupling across different frequency bands and bitrates.

Understanding Vorbis Stereo Coupling Modes

Libvorbis employs two primary coupling techniques:

  1. Polar (Mid/Side) Stereo: A lossless transformation where correlated channel data is split into magnitude and angle (sum and difference). This maintains stereo imaging without discarding spatial information.
  2. Point (Intensity) Stereo: A lossy coupling method that retains the spectral shape and overall energy distribution across channels but discards directional phase information at higher frequencies.

The "aggression" of stereo coupling refers to how low in the frequency spectrum point stereo is introduced, how easily the psychoacoustic model collapses subtle directional cues into a single envelope, and how the encoder responds to transient stereo shifts.

Core Psychoacoustic Parameters in vorbis_info_psy

Fine-grained stereo coupling settings are defined in the vorbis_info_psy structure (located internally within psy.h and the encoder setup modes). The primary variables controlling coupling behavior include:

  • coupling_point_limit: Defines the frequency floor (measured in Bark scale bins or FFT sub-bands) above which point stereo coupling is permitted. Decreasing this value forces point stereo coupling lower into the mid-range frequencies, significantly increasing coupling aggression and saving bits at the cost of spatial localization.
  • coupling_pk_limit: Sets the peak energy delta threshold between the left and right channels. If the channel disparity exceeds this limit, coupling aggression is scaled back on specific frequency peaks to prevent audible panning distortion. Lowering this threshold makes the encoder more conservative, while raising it forces aggressive channel blending even when stereo separation is pronounced.
  • coupling_pk_post_limit: Works alongside coupling_pk_limit to control the post-transient decay threshold. This parameter prevents phase collapse immediately following sharp directional attacks, ensuring that stereo separation snaps back quickly after transient bursts.
  • coupling_passlimit_ext: Sets boundaries on how many subsequent spectral passes may apply secondary coupling adjustments. Increasing pass limits allows more aggressive iterative reduction of uncorrelated high-frequency material.
  • sliding_stereo: A flag/state variable within the global psychoacoustic configuration that determines whether stereo coupling thresholds adapt dynamically based on window energy shifts or remain fixed relative to the base quality mode.

High-Level Management via Mode Templates

At the standard public API level (vorbis/vorbisenc.h), libvorbis does not provide dedicated runtime API functions to adjust these coupling parameters individually. Instead, coupling aggressiveness is mapped internally to nominal quality levels (ranging from -0.1 to 1.0 via vorbis_encode_setup_vbr or vorbis_encode_init_vbr).

The underlying parameters are configured in the static mode templates inside the libvorbis source tree (lib/modes/setup_X.h and lib/modes/psych_X.h):

  • At lower quality targets (e.g., -q 0 to -q 3), the templates lower coupling_point_limit and raise coupling_pk_limit, introducing point stereo deep into the audible mid-band.
  • At high quality targets (e.g., -q 7 and above), point stereo coupling is often disabled entirely or constrained strictly to the highest frequencies (typically above 10 kHz to 12 kHz), relying predominantly on lossless polar stereo.

Customizing stereo aggression requires modifying these psychoacoustic template arrays before calling vorbis_encode_setup_init(), or modifying the initialized vorbis_info_psy structures prior to the final encoder state allocation.
