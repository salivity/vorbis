Libvorbis -q 6 Bitrate Equivalent

In the libvorbis audio encoder, the quality setting -q 6 targets a nominal bitrate of approximately 192 kbps for standard 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz stereo audio. This article explains how the libvorbis quality scale functions, why nominal bitrates fluctuate in practice, and how -q 6 fits into the broader Vorbis encoding spectrum.

Understanding the -q 6 Quality Setting

The Vorbis format is inherently variable bitrate (VBR). When encoding audio with libvorbis, using quality-based encoding (-q or -aq) is the recommended method because it allocates bits according to psychoacoustic complexity rather than forcing a static data rate.

At -q 6, the encoder targets high-fidelity reproduction roughly equivalent to a high-quality 192 kbps MP3 or AAC stream, while maintaining transparent audio quality for the vast majority of listeners and playback equipment.

Libvorbis Quality-to-Bitrate Mapping

The libvorbis scale generally ranges from -1 (lowest) to 10 (highest). For standard two-channel stereo material at 44.1 kHz, the nominal bitrates correspond to the following settings:

  • -q 0: ~64 kbps
  • -q 1: ~80 kbps
  • -q 2: ~96 kbps
  • -q 3: ~112 kbps
  • -q 4: ~128 kbps
  • -q 5: ~160 kbps
  • -q 6: ~192 kbps
  • -q 7: ~224 kbps
  • -q 8: ~256 kbps
  • -q 9: ~320 kbps
  • -q 10: ~500 kbps

Why Actual Bitrates Vary

Because Vorbis encodes using true variable bitrate mode, the final average bitrate of a file encoded with -q 6 will rarely be exactly 192 kbps.

  • Complex Passages: Dense instrumentation, applause, or heavy percussion will demand more data and may push the local bitrate well above 200 kbps.
  • Simple Passages: Silence, spoken word, or minimal acoustic arrangements require fewer bits, causing the local bitrate to drop significantly below 192 kbps.

Command-Line Usage

To encode audio at the -q 6 setting using common command-line tools:

  • Using oggenc: oggenc -q 6 input.wav -o output.ogg

  • Using ffmpeg: ffmpeg -i input.wav -c:a libvorbis -q:a 6 output.ogg

Both commands instruct libvorbis to target the ~192 kbps quality profile while allowing dynamic bitrate allocation across the track.
