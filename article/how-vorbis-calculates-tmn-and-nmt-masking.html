How Vorbis Calculates TMN and NMT Masking

The Vorbis audio codec relies on an advanced psychoacoustic model that analyzes spectral energy distribution to differentiate between tonal components and noise-like signals. By calculating tone-masking-noise (TMN) and noise-masking-tone (NMT) parameters across psychoacoustic critical bands, Vorbis dynamically establishes masking thresholds that dictate how much quantization noise can be introduced without degrading perceptual audio quality.

Spectral Decomposition and Critical Band Mapping

Vorbis transforms time-domain audio samples into the frequency domain using a Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT), alongside an auxiliary Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) specifically dedicated to psychoacoustic estimation.

The spectral output is partitioned into critical bands modeled on the Bark scale. Human auditory frequency selectivity decreases at higher frequencies; grouping bins into Bark bands allows Vorbis to compute energy sums and masking interactions according to human auditory filter bandwidths.

Quantifying Tonality (Tone vs. Noise Discrimination)

Unlike traditional MPEG psychoacoustic models that categorize spectral lines into rigid binary classes (strictly tonal or strictly noise), Vorbis employs a continuous tonality estimation based on local spectral envelope variations and peak-to-average energy ratios:

  1. Peak Identification: The encoder searches for local maxima in the spectral magnitude representation within each critical band.
  2. Spectral Flatness and Peakiness: For each partition, Vorbis measures the ratio of the peak bin energy to the surrounding energy floor.
  3. Tonality Index (\(A\)): A continuous tonality metric bounded between 0 (pure noise-like energy) and 1 (pure sinusoidal tone) is assigned to frequency regions. A sharp, prominent spectral peak yields a high tonality index, whereas a diffuse, flat distribution yields a low tonality index.

Tone-Masking-Noise (TMN) Calculation

Tone-masking-noise occurs when a prominent single-frequency tone obscures broader, low-energy noise in surrounding spectral regions.

Because the human ear is highly sensitive to extraneous noise near pure tones, TMN requires a strict masking margin:

  • Threshold Offset: The masking threshold is placed significantly lower than the energy of the masking tone (typically 15 dB to 25 dB below the peak level).
  • Spreading Function: Vorbis applies a triangular spreading function across the Bark scale. The masking curve drops off steeply away from the center frequency of the tonal peak, with asymmetric slopes reflecting that masking extends farther into higher frequencies than lower frequencies.
  • Resulting Curve: The calculated allowable noise profile remains well below the tone's magnitude to prevent audible hiss around isolated acoustic transients or harmonic instruments.

Noise-Masking-Tone (NMT) Calculation

Noise-masking-tone describes the phenomenon where broadband, noise-like sound energy conceals a localized tone.

Noise is a more potent psychoacoustic masker than a tone:

  • Threshold Offset: The masking threshold offset for NMT is considerably shallower than for TMN (often only 3 dB to 6 dB below the masker energy level).
  • Energy Summation: Vorbis sums the diffuse energy across the critical band. The masker power is assumed to be distributed, establishing a relatively high, broad masking threshold across the entire critical band.
  • Resulting Curve: Because the masking power of noise is strong, tones within this band can tolerate larger quantization errors before becoming audible.

Parameter Interpolation and Floor Generation

Vorbis synthesizes TMN and NMT parameters into a single masking threshold curve through interpolation:

\[\text{Threshold}_{\text{final}} = A \cdot \text{Threshold}_{\text{TMN}} + (1 - A) \cdot \text{Threshold}_{\text{NMT}}\]

Where \(A\) is the tonality index of the target band.

Once this unified threshold is established across all Bark bands, Vorbis converts the masking curve into its representation known as Floor 1 (a piecewise linear approximation) or Floor 0 (an autoregressive spectral envelope). The chosen floor acts as a frequency-dependent normalization factor: the raw MDCT coefficients are divided by this floor curve, producing normalized residue values that are vector-quantized and Huffman-coded within the limits set by the TMN and NMT evaluations.
