How Does Tremor Achieve Integer-Only Vorbis Decoding?

The Tremor library (libvorbisidec) provides a fully compliant Vorbis audio decoder engineered specifically for processors lacking hardware floating-point units. By replacing IEEE 754 floating-point calculations with fixed-point math, customized lookup tables, dynamic block scaling, and precision-managed arithmetic pipelines, Tremor decodes Vorbis bitstreams with minimal computational overhead while preserving high fidelity.

Fixed-Point Arithmetic and Custom Number Formats

Standard Vorbis decoding relies heavily on fractional numbers for inverse quantization, psychoacoustic curves, and windowing. Tremor replaces floating-point values with signed 32-bit fixed-point representations. By establishing fixed binary scale factors across distinct processing stages, the decoder performs fractional multiplications and additions using standard integer registers and ALU operations. When multiplying two 32-bit fixed-point numbers, Tremor utilizes 64-bit intermediate products followed by arithmetic right shifts to maintain proper scaling and discard redundant least-significant bits without sacrificing numerical range.

Block Floating-Point and Dynamic Headroom Management

Certain stages of audio decoding—most notably the frequency-to-time transform—exhibit wide dynamic ranges that risk integer overflow or loss of subtle details. Tremor addresses this challenge by adopting block floating-point strategies. Rather than tracking individual exponents for every audio sample, the decoder analyzes an entire block of frequency coefficients, normalizes them against a shared exponent, and applies headroom shifts. This block-level scaling prevents arithmetic overflow during intensive cumulative operations while preserving quantization resolution in quieter passages.

Fast Inverse MDCT Using Integer Trigonometry

The most computationally demanding phase of Vorbis decoding is the Inverse Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (IMDCT), which transforms frequency spectra back into time-domain audio samples. The reference decoder relies on floating-point sine and cosine evaluations. Tremor replaces these operations with an integerized fast Fourier transform (FFT) kernel supported by precomputed trigonometric lookup tables. Sine and cosine values are stored as pre-scaled 16-bit or 32-bit integers. Intermediate butterfly calculations within the transform pipeline utilize carefully balanced shift steps, ensuring that accumulation does not clip before the final windowing stage.

Floor Curve Reconstruction and Codebook Dequantization

Vorbis represents spectral envelopes using two distinct floor mechanisms: Floor 0 (LSP-based) and Floor 1 (piecewise linear representation). In standard Vorbis, these curves require transcendental functions such as logarithms, square roots, and trigonometric projections. Tremor circumvents runtime transcendental computations through lookup tables that translate encoded indices directly into fixed-point representations. For Floor 1, linear interpolation across spectral bins is computed entirely via integer division and multiplication, producing smooth curves mapped straight to integer residue vectors.

Windowing and Overlap-Add Operations

Once IMDCT time-domain samples are recovered, they must be multiplied by a synthesis window function and overlapped with the preceding audio frame. Tremor precomputes the Vorbis window slope as fixed-point fractional coefficients. The synthesis multiplication reduces to integer multiply-shift steps, followed by an addition stage that writes final audio data directly into 16-bit signed pulse-code modulation (PCM) output buffers. Through this comprehensive substitution of floating-point mathematics, Tremor enables robust Vorbis playback on resource-constrained embedded microcontrollers and digital signal processors.
