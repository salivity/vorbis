What Audio Features Are Lost When Saving to Vorbis?

Saving an active audio editing project directly to the Ogg Vorbis format irreversibly strips away the core operational data required for post-production, replacing an open, editable session with a flattened, lossy master file. Because Vorbis is an end-user delivery format rather than a digital audio workstation (DAW) project file, choosing to save or export directly to it permanently destroys multi-track isolation, non-destructive editing history, active effect parameters, and original uncompressed fidelity.

Loss of Multi-Track Isolation

When a project is saved as an audio file like Vorbis, all separate tracks—such as individual instruments, vocals, sound effects, and dialogue—are mixed down into a single stereo or mono file. The ability to adjust the volume, panning, or timing of a single element independently is completely lost once the file is rendered.

Permanent Lossy Compression

Vorbis utilizes psychoacoustic lossy compression to reduce file size, meaning it permanently discards audio data that the human ear is less likely to perceive. Saving to Vorbis eliminates the original uncompressed bit-depth and high-frequency details. If this file is reopened and edited again, re-saving it will introduce generational loss, progressively degrading the audio quality with every export.

Eradication of Non-Destructive Edits and History

Working projects retain your undo history, clip boundaries, crossfades, and splice points, allowing you to slide audio clips or restore trimmed sections at any time. Saving directly to Vorbis bakes these edits permanently into a continuous waveform, eliminating the ability to alter cut points, un-trim audio, or undo previous edits.

Loss of Plugins, Instruments, and Automation

DAW projects store active VST plugins, software synthesizers, and continuous automation curves (such as volume swells or filter sweeps). When exported to Vorbis:

  • Plugin chains are finalized and cannot be tweaked, bypassed, or removed.
  • MIDI data and virtual instruments are rendered strictly as raw audio, eliminating note-level editing, pitch correction flexibility, and sound design adjustments.
  • Automation lanes are permanently merged into the audio stream, preventing any future readjustment of parameter movements.

Stripping of DAW-Specific Metadata and Markers

Project files contain structural timeline data, including tempo maps, time signature changes, arrangement markers, and cue points. Vorbis only supports standard tagging metadata (Vorbis comments) for basic information like track titles, artists, and dates. Complex arrangement data and rhythmic grids are completely dropped.

To preserve full editing capabilities, always save projects in your software's proprietary format (such as .aup3, .cpr, .flp, or .als) and reserve Vorbis strictly for the final distribution phase.
