What Character Inspired the Name of Vorbis?

The open-source audio compression format Vorbis was named after Exquisitor Vorbis, a ruthless and fundamentalist character from Terry Pratchett's acclaimed 1992 Discworld novel, Small Gods. Created by programmer Christopher "Monty" Montgomery in the late 1990s as a patent-free alternative to MP3, the technology adopted the moniker directly from the literature Montgomery was reading during the codec's formative development.

The Literary Origins in Terry Pratchett’s Small Gods

In Small Gods, the thirteenth novel in the Discworld series, Vorbis is the head of the Quisition—the brutal secret police and interrogation arm of the Omnian church. Unlike characters who act out of cynical self-interest, Vorbis is depicted as a terrifying zealot with an absolute, unyielding belief in his own righteousness, famously believing that his inner thoughts are the direct voice of the Great God Om. He possesses a photographic memory and an uncompromising nature, making him one of the most memorable and chilling antagonists in Pratchett’s satire.

How Christopher Montgomery Chose the Name

Christopher Montgomery, the lead developer behind the Xiph.Org Foundation and the creator of the Ogg container format, frequently borrowed naming inspiration from science fiction and fantasy. While coding the audio compression layer to accompany Ogg, Montgomery was reading Small Gods. He decided to name the audio format after Exquisitor Vorbis, finding the name distinctive and memorable.

The accompanying multimedia container, Ogg, was derived from the slang term "ogging," a maneuver in the text-based computer game Netrek, rather than Pratchett's character Nanny Ogg, though the shared naming often leads fans of Discworld to link the two.

Legacy in the Tech World

The Vorbis codec became a landmark in the open-source movement, providing a high-quality, royalty-free audio format that powered everything from early indie video games to major streaming infrastructure. Pratchett's literary creation thus became permanently immortalized in software history, bridging modern digital audio engineering with classic British satirical fantasy.
