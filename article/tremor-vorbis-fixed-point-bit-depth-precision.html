Tremor Vorbis Fixed-Point Bit Depth Precision

Tremor, officially known as libvorbisidec, is a fixed-point implementation of the Ogg Vorbis audio decoder tailored for embedded systems and processors that lack a hardware floating-point unit (FPU). This article provides a direct examination of the bit depth precision used in Tremor's fixed-point operations, highlighting its 32-bit internal architecture, 64-bit intermediate arithmetic, and final 16-bit audio output processing.

Primary Internal Arithmetic: 32-bit Precision

Tremor executes the vast majority of its fixed-point arithmetic using 32-bit signed integers (int32_t). Because standard Vorbis reference implementations rely heavily on 32-bit single-precision or 64-bit double-precision floating-point numbers, Tremor maps these dynamic ranges to 32-bit integer structures using fractional Q-notation:

  • Floor and Residue Vectors: Stored and computed using 32-bit fixed-point words. Depending on the stage, these values use variable radix point placement (commonly Q1.30 or dynamic scaling factors) to maximize headroom and avoid underflow.
  • Lookup Tables: Trigonometric tables and window functions are pre-computed and stored as 32-bit signed integer values.

Intermediate Math: 64-bit Accumulators

To preserve precision during multiplication, Tremor utilizes 64-bit integers (int64_t) for intermediate stages. Multiplying two 32-bit fixed-point numbers produces a 64-bit product. The algorithm immediately normalizes and bit-shifts this product back into a 32-bit container for subsequent operations. This temporary 64-bit expansion prevents clipping and arithmetic overflow in critical calculation pipelines, such as the Inverse Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (IMDCT).

IMDCT and Transform Staging

The IMDCT is the most computationally demanding stage in Vorbis decoding. Tremor implements an integer-only FFT/IMDCT engine that works strictly with 32-bit internal stage precision. It applies localized bit-shifting (block floating-point techniques) across stages to dynamically retain the highest possible precision across varying frequency magnitudes.

Final Audio Output Bit Depth

While internal synthesis operates with 32-bit precision, Tremor formats the final decoded pulse-code modulation (PCM) audio into standard signed 16-bit integer samples.

During the final overlap-add stage, the 32-bit synthesized audio samples are clamped, dithered if configured, and right-shifted to yield standard 16-bit signed PCM output, matching the standard dynamic range requirement of consumer-grade audio hardware.
