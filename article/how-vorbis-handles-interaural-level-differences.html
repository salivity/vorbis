How Vorbis Handles Interaural Level Differences

This article examines how the Vorbis audio codec encodes and preserves Interaural Level Differences (ILD) in spatial stereo recordings. By leveraging psychoacoustic modeling, channel coupling, and a specialized technique known as "point stereo," Vorbis retains the essential amplitude disparities between the left and right channels that human hearing relies on for sound localization, all while drastically minimizing bitrate requirements.

Psychoacoustics and Duplex Theory

Human spatial perception relies on Duplex Theory, which states that localization depends on Interaural Time Differences (ITD) at low frequencies (below roughly 1.5 kHz) and Interaural Level Differences (ILD) at high frequencies. Because high-frequency sound waves are shorter than the diameter of the human head, an acoustic "shadow" is cast, attenuating energy on the side opposite the sound source. Vorbis uses this biological reality to tailor its spatial compression, ensuring that amplitude disparities between channels are preserved even when fine phase details are discarded.

Spectral Decomposition: Floor and Residue

Vorbis processes audio by transforming time-domain signals into the frequency domain via a Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT). It then separates the spectral data into two distinct components:

  1. The Spectral Floor: A smoothed curve that models the broad spectral energy envelope and accounts for human auditory masking thresholds.
  2. The Residue: The detailed, fine-grained spectral peaks and harmonic structure left behind once the floor is subtracted.

Because the spectral floor represents the overall energy envelope across specific Bark scale critical bands, Vorbis can maintain independent energy profiles for the left and right channels. By quantizing the floor curves separately when necessary, the codec explicitly tracks the macro-level volume differences across frequency bins, directly preserving interaural level differences.

Channel Coupling and Point Stereo

At lower bitrates, encoding independent stereo channels (dual mono) is inefficient. To conserve bandwidth without collapsing the stereo field, Vorbis implements advanced channel coupling, specifically switching between Mid/Side (M/S) stereo and Point Stereo.

Point stereo is the primary mechanism Vorbis uses to handle ILD in complex spatial soundstages:

  • Polar Mapping: Rather than transmitting separate left and right residue vectors, Vorbis combines the fine spectral detail into a single shared magnitude vector. It then pairs this magnitude with an angular/directional value.
  • Energy Distribution: The directional value indicates the balance of power between the left and right channels for that specific frequency sub-band.
  • Preservation of ILD: During decoding, the single residue signal is scaled independently for each channel according to the directional factor and the respective channel floor curves. This ensures that the amplitude ratio—the exact ILD required for localization—is maintained across the stereo field, even though the underlying waveform detail is shared.

Dynamic Switching Across Frequency Bands

Vorbis does not apply channel coupling uniformly across the entire frequency spectrum. In spatial stereo recordings, low-frequency phase alignment (ITD) is preserved using discrete stereo or lossless Mid/Side transformations. As frequencies rise above the human phase-detection threshold, Vorbis transitions selectively into Point Stereo on a per-band, per-block basis.

This adaptive partitioning allows Vorbis to maintain precise Interaural Level Differences where they matter most to human localization, avoiding spatial collapse and image smearing while optimizing compression efficiency.
