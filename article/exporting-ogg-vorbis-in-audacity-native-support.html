Exporting Ogg Vorbis in Audacity: Native Support

Audacity provides complete native support for exporting audio into the Ogg Vorbis format without requiring any external libraries, plugins, or third-party codecs. This guide explains Audacity's built-in Ogg capabilities, the compression quality options available, and the direct steps to export your audio projects as .ogg files.

Built-In Support for Ogg Vorbis

Audacity includes the open-source libvorbis encoder within its standard installation. While proprietary formats such as M4A (AAC), WMA, or AMR require the separate FFmpeg library to function in Audacity, Ogg Vorbis works immediately out of the box on Windows, macOS, and Linux. You can both import and export .ogg files as soon as the software is installed.

Why Choose Ogg Vorbis?

Ogg Vorbis is an open-source, patent-free alternative to MP3 and AAC. It uses variable bitrate (VBR) encoding to deliver superior sound quality at smaller or comparable file sizes relative to standard MP3 files, making it widely used in game development, web streaming, and digital audio distribution.

How to Export Ogg Vorbis in Audacity

Exporting an Ogg Vorbis file takes only a few steps:

  1. Open Your Project: Load or finish editing your audio tracks in Audacity.
  2. Open the Export Menu:
    • In Audacity 3.4 and newer: Navigate to File > Export Audio...
    • In older versions: Navigate to File > Export > Export as OGG.
  3. Select Format: In the export dialog, set the Format dropdown to Ogg Vorbis Files.
  4. Configure Quality: Adjust the Quality slider to your preferred setting.
  5. Set Destination and Save: Name the file, select your destination folder, and click Export (or Save). If prompted, enter any desired metadata tags and click OK.

Understanding Ogg Vorbis Quality Settings

Audacity provides an Ogg Vorbis quality scale ranging from 0 to 10:

  • Quality 0 to 2 (~64–96 kbps): Best suited for voice recordings, audiobooks, or low-bandwidth requirements.
  • Quality 5 to 6 (~160–192 kbps): The default standard, providing near-transparent, CD-quality audio suitable for standard music listening.
  • Quality 8 to 10 (~256–500 kbps): Maximum fidelity, ideal for critical listening environments and archiving compressed audio.
