What Is the Vorbis Reference Implementation License?

The reference implementation of the Vorbis audio compression format, distributed through the libvorbis library by the Xiph.Org Foundation, is primarily released under the permissive revised 3-Clause BSD License. This licensing structure grants developers and commercial entities wide latitude to encode, decode, modify, and redistribute Vorbis-compatible software without incurring royalty fees or facing restrictive copyleft obligations.

The License Behind libvorbis

The core library containing the standard reference encoder and decoder—known as libvorbis—along with auxiliary modules like libvorbisfile and libvorbisenc, is governed by the 3-Clause BSD License. This permissive open-source license permits both non-commercial and proprietary applications to link against or bundle the code. The requirements are minimal: redistributions of source or binary forms must retain the copyright notice, maintain the brief list of conditions, and avoid using the names of the foundation or its contributors to endorse derived products without prior permission.

Specification vs. Implementation Licensing

Understanding the Vorbis ecosystem requires distinguishing between the implementation and the audio format specification itself:

  • The Format Specification: The Vorbis codec specification belongs to the public domain. Anyone is legally permitted to create an independent encoder or decoder from scratch without paying licensing fees or signing proprietary agreements.
  • The Reference Libraries: Software packages like libvorbis and the fixed-point embedded decoder Tremor are distributed under the 3-Clause BSD License.
  • Command-Line Tools: Ancillary user-facing utilities included in the separate vorbis-tools suite (such as oggenc and ogg123) are generally governed by the GNU General Public License (GPL).

The Transition From LGPL to BSD

The reference implementation was not always licensed under the BSD framework. In its initial development stages, libvorbis was released under the GNU Lesser General Public License (LGPL). In February 2001, the Xiph.Org Foundation chose to re-license the library under the 3-Clause BSD License.

The primary motivation was to encourage widespread, friction-free industry adoption. A BSD license eliminated legal hurdles for proprietary software vendors and game studios who were wary of dynamic linking requirements under copyleft licenses. This strategic shift received the explicit endorsement of Free Software Foundation founder Richard Stallman, who recognized that unencumbered adoption of Vorbis was vital to competing with proprietary, patent-encumbered formats like MP3 and AAC.
