Libvorbis -q 0 Nominal Bitrate Guide

This article provides an overview of the libvorbis -q 0 audio quality setting, detailing its corresponding nominal bitrate, how the libvorbis encoding scale functions, and the practical use cases for this low-bitrate compression level.

The Nominal Bitrate for libvorbis -q 0

In libvorbis, the quality setting -q 0 corresponds to a nominal bitrate of approximately 64 kbps (kilobits per second) for a standard stereo track at 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz.

Because libvorbis operates using Variable Bitrate (VBR) by default, 64 kbps is a target average rather than a strict limit. The encoder dynamically allocates more bits to complex passages and fewer bits to simpler or silent passages. Depending on the complexity and dynamic range of the source audio, the actual resulting bitrate may fluctuate above or below 64 kbps.

The libvorbis Quality Scale

Libvorbis uses a floating-point quality scale typically ranging from -1 (or -0.1) up to 10. Setting quality via the -q switch is preferred over specifying an explicit target bitrate (-b), as VBR encoding preserves perceptual consistency across the entire audio file.

Below is a quick reference for low-to-medium libvorbis quality presets for stereo audio:

  • -q -1: ~45 kbps
  • -q 0: ~64 kbps
  • -q 1: ~80 kbps
  • -q 2: ~96 kbps
  • -q 3: ~112 kbps
  • -q 4: ~128 kbps

When to Use -q 0

A nominal bitrate of 64 kbps provides acceptable quality for scenarios where bandwidth or storage constraints are critical:

  • Spoken Word Content: Podcasts, audiobooks, and voice recordings remain clear and intelligible at this setting.
  • Low-Bandwidth Streaming: Suitable for mobile or slow internet connections where conserving data takes precedence over high-fidelity reproduction.
  • Background Game Audio: Lightweight ambient effects and dialogue in mobile or web-based game assets.

For general music listening where higher fidelity is desired, settings between -q 4 (~128 kbps) and -q 6 (~192 kbps) are standard recommendations.
