Multiplexing Vorbis Audio with Dirac Video

Yes, Vorbis audio can be multiplexed with Dirac video streams. Because both codecs were designed with open-source and royalty-free multimedia ecosystems in mind, their integration is fully supported across flexible multimedia container formats like Ogg and Matroska. This article explains how these two streams are combined, the primary containers used, and the tools required to multiplex them into a single playable file.

Supported Container Formats

A raw video stream and an audio stream cannot play simultaneously without a multiplexer (muxer) to interleave them into a single container format. Dirac and Vorbis share high compatibility within open containers:

  • The Ogg Container (.ogg, .ogv): The Ogg container is the native home for Vorbis and a primary target format for Dirac. The BBC (creators of Dirac) and the Xiph.Org Foundation (creators of Vorbis and Ogg) established formal mapping specifications to encapsulate Dirac video packets alongside Vorbis audio as concurrent logical bitstreams. Inside an Ogg stream, timing and synchronization are managed natively through granule positions.
  • Matroska (.mkv): Matroska is an open-standard container designed to accept virtually any codec combination. Matroska natively supports Dirac video and Vorbis audio, providing reliable timecode synchronization, chapter support, and subtitle handling.

Synchronization and Stream Handling

When multiplexing Vorbis with Dirac, the muxer interleaves packets from both streams while maintaining accurate timestamps to ensure audio-video synchronization (lip-sync):

  • Framing and Headers: The container stores the distinct initialization headers for both streams. For Vorbis, this includes codebooks and identification headers; for Dirac, this involves sequence headers defining frame rates, picture dimensions, and colorimetry.
  • Packet Interleaving: Video frames compressed with Dirac (which utilizes wavelet compression) and audio frames compressed with Vorbis are buffered and sequenced chronologically based on their presentation timestamps (PTS).

Tools for Multiplexing

Standard open-source media utilities can multiplex Dirac video and Vorbis audio streams without requiring re-encoding:

  • FFmpeg: The versatile command-line utility supports both codecs. If the build includes libschroedinger or native Dirac/Vorbis parsers, streams can be joined into an MKV or Ogg container using the stream copy mode: ffmpeg -i video_input.dirac -i audio_input.ogg -c copy output.mkv
  • GStreamer: A multimedia framework that includes muxing elements like oggmux and matroskamux, capable of taking a Dirac video source and a Vorbis audio source and combining them in a processing pipeline.
  • MKVToolNix: A suite of tools tailored for Matroska that can directly package raw Dirac elementary streams and Vorbis audio tracks into an MKV file.
