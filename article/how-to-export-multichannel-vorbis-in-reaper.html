How to Export Multichannel Vorbis in REAPER

REAPER natively supports exporting multichannel OGG Vorbis audio tracks without requiring external encoders, third-party software, or additional codecs. This guide explains how REAPER handles multichannel Vorbis files, provides a straightforward step-by-step process to set up your master bus and render settings, and outlines practical use cases such as surround sound mixing and game audio production.

Native Vorbis Support in REAPER

Cockos REAPER includes built-in encoding libraries for Ogg Vorbis. Unlike some digital audio workstations that restrict OGG output to standard mono or stereo configurations, REAPER allows you to define custom channel counts directly in the render engine. You can encode discrete 4-channel (quadraphonic), 6-channel (5.1 surround), 8-channel (7.1 surround), or arbitrary multichannel layouts directly to a single .ogg container.

Preparing the Multichannel Routing

Before rendering, ensure your project routing can deliver discrete multichannel audio to the output:

  1. Open the Master Track Routing by clicking the Route button on the Master track in the Mixer or Track Control Panel.
  2. Locate the Track Channels dropdown menu.
  3. Change the channel count from the default 2 to your target output count (e.g., 4 for quad, 6 for 5.1, or 8 for 7.1).
  4. Route your individual tracks or buses to the corresponding master channels (e.g., Channels 1–2 for Front L/R, 3 for Center, 4 for LFE, 5–6 for Surrounds).

Step-by-Step Render Process

Once your mix is routed correctly, export the multichannel file using the native render dialog:

  1. Go to the top menu and select File > Render (or press Ctrl+Alt+R on Windows / Cmd+Opt+R on macOS).
  2. Under the Output section, specify your directory and file name.
  3. In the Options section, find the Channels dropdown:
    • Select the exact number of channels matching your master track setup (e.g., 4, 6, or 8).
  4. In the Primary Output Format section, open the format dropdown and choose OGG Vorbis.
  5. Configure the Vorbis settings:
    • Choose your preferred encoding mode: VBR (Quality-based) or CBR (Bitrate-based).
    • Adjust the quality slider or bitrate value according to your project requirements.
  6. Click Render 1 File.

REAPER will generate a single .ogg file containing all discrete audio streams within the container.

Common Use Cases

  • Game Audio Middleware: Engines like Unity and Unreal, as well as middleware such as Wwise and FMOD, commonly utilize multichannel OGG Vorbis for ambient loops, interactive music stems, and localized surround sound.
  • Surround Sound Distribution: Independent distribution of 5.1 and quadraphonic music often relies on OGG Vorbis due to its royalty-free nature, small file size, and broad support across open-source media players.
