Sample-Accurate Seeking in Ogg Vorbis Explained

This article provides a technical overview of how sample-accurate seeking operates in Vorbis audio streams. It details the interaction between the Ogg container and the Vorbis audio codec, focusing on the granule position timestamping mechanism, page-based bisection searching, and the preroll decoding required to reconstruct continuous audio waveforms down to an exact PCM sample.

The Role of the Ogg Granule Position

Vorbis is an audio coding format, but it is almost universally encapsulated within the Ogg container format. The primary mechanism enabling sample accuracy is the Ogg container's granulepos (granule position) field.

Every Ogg page header contains a 64-bit granulepos integer. In a Vorbis stream, this number does not represent a timestamp in milliseconds; instead, it stores the exact, cumulative number of PCM audio samples decoded up to the end of the last complete packet in that page. Because this count represents absolute sample numbers from the beginning of the audio track, it provides an absolute reference frame unaffected by variable bitrates.

Bisection Search Algorithm

Because Ogg Vorbis files are typically streamed or stored without a global index table or seek table at the start of the file, decoders locate target samples using a bisection (binary) search:

  1. Boundary Probing: The decoder inspects the beginning and end of the file to determine the total duration and starting sample offset.
  2. Interpolation and Bisecting: To find target sample \(N\), the decoder estimates byte offsets and performs binary searches across the file, reading the granulepos stored in the headers of Ogg pages.
  3. Pinpointing the Target Page: The search narrows down until it locates the specific Ogg page where the previous page ends at a granule position less than \(N\), and the current page ends at a granule position greater than or equal to \(N\).

Overcoming Overlap-Add and MDCT Artifacts

Finding the correct data packet is not enough for sample accuracy due to the transform properties of the Vorbis codec. Vorbis relies on the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) with overlapping frames (typically short and long windows of varying lengths, such as 256 or 2048 samples).

Each decoded packet produces half the data required for an audio frame, with the remaining half coming from the overlap-add of the preceding and subsequent packets:

  • Preroll Decoding: A decoder cannot simply start rendering audio directly from the packet containing sample \(N\). Doing so would result in audio discontinuities (clicks or incorrect amplitude) because the overlap buffer is empty.
  • Preceding Window Fetch: The decoder must seek to at least one packet (or more, depending on block switching sequences) prior to the target packet to populate the overlap-add synthesis window.

Final Sample Trimming

Once the decoder initializes its synthesis buffer by decoding the preceding preroll packet, it decodes the packet containing the target sample \(N\). The decoder then performs the overlap-add calculation to reconstruct the PCM waveform and discards all synthesized samples prior to \(N\). The resulting output begins precisely at the requested PCM sample index, completing the process of sample-accurate seeking.
